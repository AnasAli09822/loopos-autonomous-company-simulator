# Two-Year Thesis

Over the next two years, the important shift in autonomous-company software will not be “more chatbots.” It will be the move from disconnected AI tools to accountable operating systems where autonomous roles share state, act inside explicit authority, and leave durable evidence.

Today, many business workflows still depend on a person copying context between CRM, spreadsheets, ticketing, finance systems, and messaging tools. Agentic systems can remove much of that coordination latency, but only if they operate on a common record and cannot silently impersonate one another. A Sales agent should not be able to create cash, an Operations agent should not be able to approve its own spend, and a Finance agent should not be able to invent commercial commitments.

The human role will therefore move upward: away from routine workflow execution and toward policy, risk appetite, exceptions, and genuinely ambiguous judgment. The best systems will know when to continue autonomously and when to stop.

Replay, event histories, authority boundaries, and state-level controls will become as important as model quality. The companies that benefit most will not be those with the most agents, but those whose feedback loops are fast, observable, reversible where possible, and accountable when they are not.

LoopOS models that operating pattern in miniature.
